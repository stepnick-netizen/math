Monomials on one page

Hi! Bulya, Prickles, Muddle and I have drawn everything important about monomials. The arrows show you what goes where.

1

Powers: a quick reminder

A power is a short way to write repeated multiplication: a³ = a × a × a. When you multiply powers, add the indices.

2

What is a monomial?

A monomial is a single term: numbers, letters and their powers multiplied together. Only ×, no + or −.

These are monomials too: 7, x, y³, −ab, even 0. A minus in front belongs to the number, so it is fine.

A monomial is like me and Ponya holding paws. Put a plus between us and we are two separate guinea pigs!

3

The big rule: simplest form

① Multiply all the numbers and put the answer first. ② Collect each letter once: add its indices.

Number at the front, each letter only once. Just like my tidy cupboard!

4

The coefficient

The number at the front of a monomial in simplest form. If you cannot see a number, a 1 is hiding there.

An invisible 1 always sits in front of the letters. Sometimes with a minus!

5

The degree of a monomial

Add up the indices of all the letters. Ignore the number at the front.

c means c¹. A number on its own (like 7) has degree 0.

6

Multiplying monomials

Multiply the numbers together. Combine matching letters: add the indices.

Check: the degree of the answer equals the degrees added. 3 + 4 = 7, and 12a³b⁴ has degree 7 too.

7

A monomial to a power

The index outside the bracket goes to every factor inside. Multiply the letters' indices.

The index outside the bracket is like an umbrella: it covers everyone inside.

A minus disappears with an even power: (−2)² = 4. It stays with an odd power: (−2)³ = −8.

8

Working backwards

Finding a missing factor? Divide the numbers, subtract the indices. Finding what was squared? Halve the indices.

For a cube, divide the indices by 3 and find the number that, times itself three times, gives the one you need.

9

Monomials in real life

A new run for Ponya and Bulya: 3a long and 2a wide. Its area is a monomial!

If a = 1 m, the area is 6 m². If a = 2 m, it is 6 × 4 = 24 m².

Check yourself: one number at the front, each letter just once. Then it is all tidy and Prickles is happy!
