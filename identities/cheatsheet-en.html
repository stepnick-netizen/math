Identities and expressions on one page

Hi! Here is the revision before the test: identities, using a "block", the smallest value, comparing powers and proofs.

Lesson and game · Українська версія · PDF to print

1

Whole expressions

A whole expression is built from numbers and letters with +, −, ×, powers and division by a number.

A monomial is the simplest whole expression: multiplication only.

2

An identity

An identity is an equation that is true for every value of the letters. For example, 2(a + 3) = 2a + 6.

In UK textbooks an identity is written with the sign ≡.

A table is only a check. You prove it by changing the expression.

3

Key rule: how to prove an identity

Change one side step by step until it looks exactly like the other. ① Expand the brackets. ② Collect like terms.

Step by step, no jumps, and nobody can doubt it!

4

One counterexample is enough

To show an equation is not an identity, one set of numbers where it fails is enough.

I thought (a + b)² = a² + b². One example, and it’s clear!

5

Use the block

Know the value of a whole "block"? Build the expression you need from it: multiply it or raise it to a power.

To spot the block, split the numbers: 4 = 2², 6 = 3 × 2.

6

The smallest value

A square (and any even power) is never negative: x² ≥ 0. So (x − 1)² + 3 ≥ 3.

The smallest value happens when the bracket is zero: x = 1.

7

Comparing powers

Rewrite the powers with the same index (or the same base), and comparing becomes easy.

So 2¹⁵ < 3¹⁰. Check: 32,768 < 59,049.

8

Proving divisibility

The sum of three consecutive whole numbers is divisible by 3. We prove it for any n, not just a few examples.

We took out the common factor 3, so it divides by 3.

9

Identities in real life

A fence round a pen: you can go "length + width" twice, or add two lengths and two widths. Same answer: that’s an identity.

Identities let you choose the easier way and be sure the answer won’t change.

Check yourself: put one or two numbers into your answer. Don’t match? Look for the mistake. Match? Prove it by rewriting.
