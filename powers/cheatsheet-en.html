Powers on one page

Hi! Here is everything important about powers: what they are and three rules that make hard sums easy. Follow the arrows.

Lesson and game · Українська версія · PDF to print

1

What a power is

A power is a short way to write repeated multiplication: aⁿ = a × a × … × a (n times).

a¹ = a: one factor is just the number. Say it: “two to the power of five”.

2

Minus signs and brackets

A negative number to an even power turns positive; to an odd power it stays negative.

−2² means −(2²): the power only belongs to the 2. To include the minus, use brackets.

Brackets are like a hug: everything inside gets the power.

3

Key rule: multiplying powers

Same base? Add the indices: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ.

This only works for the same base: you can’t do it with 2³ × 5².

Same base? Put them all in one group and count!

4

Dividing powers

Same base? Subtract the indices: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (a ≠ 0).

Check: 243 ÷ 9 = 27.

Cancel the pairs and there’s less work to do!

5

A power of a power

Raise a power to a power? Multiply the indices: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ.

Don’t mix them up: a² × a³ = a⁵ (add), but (a²)³ = a⁶ (multiply).

6

A power of a product

The index outside the brackets goes to every factor: (ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ.

It works backwards too: 25² × 4² = (25 × 4)² = 100² = 10,000. Same index? Put them under one bracket.

The index outside is an umbrella over everyone.

7

Change to the same base

Different numbers are often powers of the same base. Rewrite them, and the rules work again.

8² × 4 = (23)² × 22 = 26 × 22 = 28 = 256

Handy to know: 9 = 3², 27 = 3³, 49 = 7², 100 = 10², 1000 = 10³.

8

Simplify first, then work it out

Don’t multiply out huge numbers. Combine the indices first, then work out a small power.

7⁸ is over 5 million. Why bother, when the answer is 49?

9

Powers in real life

Fold a sheet of paper in half and there are twice as many layers. Fold again: twice as many again.

Five folds make 2⁵ = 32 layers. Ten make 2¹⁰ = 1024. Rumours spread just as fast when everyone tells two friends.

Check yourself: same base? Multiplying: add the indices. Dividing: subtract them. Power of a power: multiply them.
